MetaCRiSP 2027


2nd Workshop on Metascience and Critical Reflections in Security & Privacy


Co-located with the 48th IEEE Security and Privacy (S&P) Symposium

May 20, 2027 · Montreal, Canada

The MetaCRiSP Workshop aims to create a dedicated venue for metascience, philosophy of science, and critical reflections of the research practices and evaluation processes that shape our field. It is focusing on our very practices of conducting research as well as our procedures of reviewing and accepting research works. To that end, this workshop invites the security and privacy community to take a reflective pause:

How do we conduct, evaluate, and sustain our own research?

Our peer-review practices are changing faster than our understanding of them. Recently, IEEE S&P 2026 introduced an interactive rebuttal phase, bridging the gap between rebuttal and revision. USENIX Security 2026 abolished major revisions, made artifact availability at submission mandatory, and required an ethics reflection. Each change is a response to the growth and expectations of the community, and program chairs have started to document the reasoning behind such decisions in transparency reports. However, introducing a policy alone does not tell us whether it achieves its intended effect, and, surprisingly, the review process itself has rarely been studied scientifically. Beyond peer review, a growing body of research examines how security and privacy knowledge is produced, evaluated, and shared, highlighting opportunities to better understand and improve research practices. This includes examining research methods, evaluation approaches, reproducibility, ethical considerations, and broader practices that shape the security and privacy research community. Critical reflection on how we conduct security research is essential to achieving validity, fairness, and long-term scientific soundness.

Meta-research—research about research—allows us, as a community, to examine trends in our research and make informed decisions regarding the course of our future research activities.

– Baset, A., & Denning, T. (2019)

Our community initiated this discussion with the first MetaCRiSP workshop at IEEE S&P 2026 (see our workshop report), yet many questions remain open and changes to research and evaluation practices often take time before their effects can be meaningfully observed. MetaCRiSP 2027 continues these discussions and aims to sustain an open and continuous dialogue between what we learn about our research and peer review practices, how we critically reflect on it, and how we act on it.

We welcome participation from researchers, reviewers, program committee members, artifact and ethics evaluators, and practitioners across academia, industry, and government.

Call for Papers


MetaCRiSP invites all contributions on metascience in the field of security and privacy. In particular, we invite works that critically reflect on the following topics of interest, including but not limited to:

Evaluation Processes

  • Procedural aspects of evaluating research (“peer review”)
  • Artifact evaluation processes and its challenges
  • Formalized ethics and open science reviews
  • “Science of Security” and community norms

Meta-Research on Research Practices

  • Methodological choices (e.g., design, analysis, implementation, or evaluation of research, including used datasets, metrics, or evaluation practices) and their implications for validity and impact
  • Reproducibility and replicability of security and privacy research
  • Availability and long-term accessibility of research artifacts, data, and code
  • Ethical considerations and reflexivity in study design and execution

Submission Guidelines

All papers must be original and not simultaneously submitted to another workshop or conference. The following submission types are welcome:

Research Paper: Up to 8 pages (including references and appendices). Research papers are peer-reviewed by the program committee and will be published in the workshop’s proceedings. Accepted papers are presented as short research spotlights grouped around common themes and followed by moderated discussion. Research papers include (but are not limited to):

  • Full Research Paper: Novel research on metascience in security and privacy.
  • Preliminary Work Paper: Early insights from interesting and new ideas.
  • Extended Work Paper: Unpublished aspects of previously published work on metascience topics (need to link to original publication and provide a brief one-page summary).
  • Experience Paper: Lessons learned from conducting metascience research.

Position Provocation: Up to 2 pages introducing a contested or emerging issue, or an open question. Their purpose is to kickstart a discussion at the workshop: Authors give a short introductory talk of up to 5 minutes, followed by an open discussion with the audience. Position provocations are lightly reviewed by the program chairs for relevance and quality. They are not published in the proceedings but will be released on the workshop’s website.

The MetaCRiSP'27 proceedings will be published post-conference in the 2027 IEEE Security & Privacy Workshop (SPW) proceedings. At least one author of each accepted paper must register for the workshop and present the paper. Presentations are expected to be in-person.

Formatting instructions (following IEEE S&P):

  • The review process is double-blind.
  • All submissions must be anonymized.
  • All papers must be formatted for US letter (not A4) size paper.
  • All submissions must use the IEEE “compsoc” conference proceedings template (\documentclass[conference,compsoc]{IEEEtran}).

Important note: Simultaneous submission of the same work to multiple venues, submission of previously published work (except for extended work papers), and plagiarism are prohibited. Contact the program chairs for any questions.
